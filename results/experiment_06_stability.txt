Experiment 6: Stability of Layer-Wise Probing

Model:
DistilBERT (distilbert-base-uncased)

Dataset:
500 sentences across 5 semantic categories.
100 sentences per category.

Method:
1. Extracted hidden states from all 7 DistilBERT layers.
2. Used attention-mask weighted mean pooling.
3. Used 5 different stratified 80/20 train-test splits.
4. Trained a Logistic Regression classifier separately on each layer.
5. Calculated mean accuracy and standard deviation across the 5 splits.

Results:

Layer 0:
Mean accuracy: 0.948
Standard deviation: 0.02135

Layer 1:
Mean accuracy: 0.942
Standard deviation: 0.02713

Layer 2:
Mean accuracy: 0.946
Standard deviation: 0.01855

Layer 3:
Mean accuracy: 0.940
Standard deviation: 0.02191

Layer 4:
Mean accuracy: 0.948
Standard deviation: 0.02040

Layer 5:
Mean accuracy: 0.944
Standard deviation: 0.01625

Layer 6:
Mean accuracy: 0.944
Standard deviation: 0.01625

Observation:
Mean classification accuracy remained within a relatively narrow range across all seven layers, from 0.940 to 0.948. The results did not show a monotonic increase in classification accuracy with layer depth.

Interpretation:
The stability experiment indicates that the layer-wise probing results are not strongly dependent on a single train-test split. Category information remained recoverable across all layers, including the initial representation. This supports the earlier observation that deeper layers do not simply show progressively higher category classification accuracy.

Limitation:
The dataset is manually constructed and the experiment uses five train-test splits. Therefore, the results should still be considered preliminary and may not generalize to other datasets or tasks.
