Experiment 3: Layer-Wise Category Separation on a Larger Dataset

Model:
DistilBERT (distilbert-base-uncased)

Dataset:
50 sentences across 5 semantic categories.
10 sentences per category.

Categories:
Sports, Animals, Technology, Food, Travel

Method:
1. Tokenized 50 sentences using the DistilBERT tokenizer.
2. Extracted hidden states from all 7 layers.
3. Used attention-mask weighted mean pooling.
4. Calculated pairwise cosine similarity.
5. Calculated within-category and between-category similarity.
6. Calculated separation as:
   Within-category similarity - Between-category similarity.

Results:

Layer 0:
Within-category similarity: 0.68482465
Between-category similarity: 0.63235710
Separation: 0.05246753

Layer 1:
Within-category similarity: 0.80963340
Between-category similarity: 0.75155115
Separation: 0.05808222

Layer 2:
Within-category similarity: 0.86788370
Between-category similarity: 0.82926850
Separation: 0.03861517

Layer 3:
Within-category similarity: 0.89739950
Between-category similarity: 0.86480340
Separation: 0.03259611

Layer 4:
Within-category similarity: 0.90312520
Between-category similarity: 0.86843950
Separation: 0.03468573

Layer 5:
Within-category similarity: 0.86727077
Between-category similarity: 0.80756660
Separation: 0.05970419

Layer 6:
Within-category similarity: 0.74349725
Between-category similarity: 0.64099290
Separation: 0.10250437

Observation:
Within-category similarity was higher than between-category similarity at every layer. Category separation increased substantially in the later layers, with the largest separation observed at Layer 6.

Limitation:
The dataset contains only 50 manually constructed sentences across five broad semantic categories. The categories may contain lexical cues that make separation easier. Therefore, these results should be considered preliminary rather than a general conclusion about transformer representations.
