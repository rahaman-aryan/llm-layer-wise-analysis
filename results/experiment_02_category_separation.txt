Experiment 2: Within-Category vs Between-Category Similarity

Model:
DistilBERT (distilbert-base-uncased)

Number of layers:
7 (Layer 0 = initial representation, Layers 1-6 = Transformer layers)

Dataset:
6 sentences from 3 categories:
- Sports
- Animals
- Technology

Method:
1. Tokenized the sentences using the DistilBERT tokenizer.
2. Extracted hidden states from every layer.
3. Used attention-mask-weighted mean pooling to obtain one vector per sentence.
4. Calculated pairwise cosine similarity between sentence representations.
5. Separated sentence pairs into within-category and between-category pairs.
6. Calculated the average similarity for both groups.
7. Calculated the separation score as:
   Separation = Within-category similarity - Between-category similarity

Between-category similarity: 0.57588970
Separation: 0.08062506

Within-category similarity: 0.75778130
Between-category similarity: 0.59803480
Separation: 0.15974653

Initial observation:
Within-category similarity was higher than between-category similarity at every layer. The largest separation was observed at Layer 6, followed by Layer 5.

Interpretation:
In this preliminary experiment, later DistilBERT layers showed greater separation between the predefined semantic categories. This suggests that category-related information may become more distinguishable in the later layers.

Limitation:
The experiment uses only 6 sentences, with 2 sentences per category. Therefore, the results should be treated as a preliminary observation rather than a general conclusion about DistilBERT.

Future improvement:
Use a larger and more diverse dataset with more examples per category and evaluate whether the observed layer-wise pattern remains consistent.
